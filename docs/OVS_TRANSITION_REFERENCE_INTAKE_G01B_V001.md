## Editorial classification before picking an effect
1. **Cut relationships** — subject, action/movement, audio, conceptual and graphic/shape match cuts. These are editorial relationships between shots, often visible only through the cut itself. **Existing**: B, D and O categories.
2. **Time overlap** — cross dissolves and other optical blends. **Existing**: E family.
3. **Compositional coexistence** — collage/mixed-media and split-screen treatments; this can be a shot format, montage design, or transition. **Expanded**: H09 + new P01–P08.
4. **Image transformation** — morphing and semantic changes can be shape/texture operations or neural image synthesis. **Existing with new restriction**: I10, N08. Face identity morphing is **not** canon-safe by default.
5. **Perceptual interruption** — strobes and inversion. **Existing with separate safety gate**: G04 and adjacent light/exposure effects.

## New P-family: eight distinct implementation patterns
- **TR-P01 — Layered cutout collage bridge (T1).** Rotoscoped subjects, designed shapes, and independent footage layers overlap then replace the base scene. **Why:** Editorial portraiture, chorus visualization, parallel memory or image-media contrast. **Quality risk:** Separate masks, intentional matte edges, cohesive shadow/perspective/texture; verify provenance and releases.
- **TR-P02 — Still-photo flipbook handoff (T1).** A rapid, controlled progression of still frames develops movement before a selected still becomes live action. **Why:** Show time, growth or a performance change without synthetic motion. **Quality risk:** Anchor frame registered; preserve temporal readability; inspect rapid luminance changes.
- **TR-P03 — Practical tear / paper reveal (T1).** A photographed or hand-animated paper edge exposes the incoming scene as an actual layer. **Why:** Tactile analog section changes and documentary/editorial motifs. **Quality risk:** Paper grain, shadow direction, matte continuity, image rights and no stock-looking wipe.
- **TR-P04 — Split-screen panel birth (T1).** A single coherent frame grows into two or more distinct views by motivated edge, subject or action division. **Why:** Compare concurrent locations or reveal unseen reactions. **Quality risk:** Panel count and crop readable on mobile; no false continuity or disallowed comparison.
- **TR-P05 — Multi-panel collapse to hero frame (T1).** Multiple panels contract or join into one authored hero shot, synchronized to a meaningful image or music cue. **Why:** Resolve competing perspectives and re-focus the audience. **Quality risk:** Match hero frame native aspect/scale; panel boundaries never obscure key performance.
- **TR-P06 — Diegetic display / nested-screen relay (T1).** An in-world display, window, printed image or projection becomes a frame inside the edit and can occupy the final canvas. **Why:** Move between mediated viewpoint and direct lived scene. **Quality risk:** Screen content source and clearance; parallax, resolution, reflection and viewing-angle logic.
- **TR-P07 — Multi-frame call-and-response montage (T1).** Two or more independent panels alternate attention through timing, action alignment or complementary sound. **Why:** Music-video ensembles, separated performance, simultaneous choices. **Quality risk:** Do not let split panels reduce facial readability or defeat musical phrasing.
- **TR-P08 — Collage-to-live-action deconstruction (T2).** A layered collage sequentially strips or resolves into an uninterrupted source plate. **Why:** Introduce a stylized interior perception then return to grounded physical reality. **Quality risk:** Final plate must exist; identity and lighting remain authorized; synthetic merges prohibited without approval.

## Three production architecture recipes

### Recipe A — Film: two character viewpoints, no false identity implication
Shot 1: authorized subject established in actual scene; hold enough information for character intent. Shot 2: different setting with deliberately similar face framing / gaze direction, cut on emotional reaction. Shot 3: sound carries through to a third place, but remains justified in-story. Shot 4: plain action / reaction shot that breaks the formal match once the audience understands the comparison. Use normal performance direction and camera matching. If this is SECOND HAND, do **not** revive retired Recorder A/B, predictive audio, Twelve Photographs, tapping, hand-correction, displacement or evidence-puzzle devices. Preserve existing identity authority and source shot locks. Not canon until film-specific approval.

### Recipe B — Music video: collage build and clean hero reveal
Start from a licensed live-action portrait plate. Add separately cleared stills or hand-designed physical textures as 2D layers, one at a time. Allow independent crop movement and optical breathing; never let the mask alter the artist's face. On a real musical phrase marker, convert layered fragments into 2–3 asymmetric panels; at the resolution point expand one authorized live-action plate to full-screen. Support a six-shot graphic/action match chain if footage exists. Test A: straight-cut version, B: moderate layer version, C: full multi-frame construction. Measure hook clarity and visibility on mobile.

### Recipe C — Surreal scene: transformation without identity mutation
Start from a practical architectural or object match. Use a true camera/shape relationship or masked blend to connect locations. If an image transformation is essential, use neutral scene texture / practical inanimate objects; for any living-person face or anatomical morph, require explicit source authority, separate approval and frame-by-frame human QC. A slower cross dissolve is not a safer replacement if it implies an unauthorized character transformation.

## Required technical sheet for all G01B work
- **Editorial / story authority:** the purpose of the transition must be stated in one factual sentence. Picture-only and with-sound A/B tests. If the clean cut is better, use it.
- **Picture format:** target 16:9, 2.39:1, 9:16 or other aspect must be declared before panel layout. Inspect small-screen portrait / landscape crops separately.
- **Source registration:** asset ID, licensing status, fps, color space, lens/composition, camera move, subject and identity authority, wardrobe, chronology, approved transformations.
- **Collage layers:** source layer ID, transparency/matte, depth and light, grain/halation/edge treatment, expected motion and exit strategy. Flat pasted-on textures are a failure unless a deliberate printed-paper aesthetic is the concept.
- **Split screen:** number of panels, timeline parity, actor visibility, mask edge, panel transform, where the eye should travel. **No auto-generated extra people in panels**.
- **Dissolve:** duration must be editorially selected, not preset by default; check two faces overlapping, spatial misleadingness, ghosting and grade consistency.
- **Morph:** use verified endpoints; track objects and faces independently. Neural morph of face/body/persona cannot silently override canonical locked identity. Label AI-assisted variants separately, provenance retained.
- **Strobe/inversions:** default to non-flashing alternatives. For web distribution, W3C WCAG 2.2 SC 2.3.1 sets a criterion of at most three flashes per second **or** flashes remaining below defined general/red-flash thresholds; passing that web standard is not a blanket theatrical/broadcast safety certification. Analyze largest playback size, HDR version as applicable, and loops, including transitions across loop seams. A warning card is not a substitute for complying with the relevant thresholds. Avoid saturated-red flash motifs whenever possible.
- **Delivery:** final frame-by-frame and audio inspection, source/right-clearance report, light-flash inspection, character-identity check, typography/logo clearance, A/B/C comparison and human approval record.
- **Cost boundary:** no paid video generation, no public site change, no production merge without authorization.

## Project recommendations — provisional only
**SECOND HAND:** Prefer B subject/behavior/action matching, selective E dissolves only on purposeful time/tone shifts. Collage is experimental and not a default dramatic engine. No retired mechanics.
**THE LAST ANIMAL:** Use conceptual, reaction and location matches; restrained mixed-media for an authorized research/documentary language, not new fictional evidence.
**Music videos:** Test P01/P04/P05/P08, B01, O01/O08, D04. Avoid defaulting to strobe-heavy editing.
