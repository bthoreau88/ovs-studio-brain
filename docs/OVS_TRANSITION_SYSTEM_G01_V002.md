> STUDIO PRINCIPLE: A transition earns its place through a change in meaning, emotion, spatial understanding, time, musical relationship, or character behavior. If the designed version does not outperform a clean cut in context, use the cut.

## Executive research conclusion
This registry catalogs **192 distinct techniques/variants in 15 families**, with production use, failure mode and execution route. It is broad but **not a mathematically exhaustive list**: editors combine techniques and invent hybrids continuously. Do not mistake built-in transition presets for all transition grammar. [S01–S05]

The highest-value grammar for OVS narratives is performance, reaction, J/L sound, elliptical cuts and carefully motivated match cuts. Music videos may use more graphical and material transformations, but movement continuity, musical phrase and analog surface authority remain the controls. Walter Murch’s editorial priority model places emotion and story ahead of technical continuity, and cognitive research finds that differing degrees of discontinuity influence how viewers segment events. [S02–S03]

## Editorial hierarchy to lock into OVS
- **T0 / Default:** Straight cut; cut on action; eyeline; reaction; J/L cuts; narrative ellipsis; graphic/conceptual/behavior match.
- **T1 / Designed physical:** Whip, door/pillar occlusion, lens cover, rack focus, prism, practical flare, luma reveal, controlled dissolve.
- **T2 / Directed experimental:** Slit-scan, stop-motion, analog emulsion, pixilation, time echo, depth/portal, datamosh, 3D camera projections.
- **T3 / R&D only:** Generative intermediate frames, neural morphs, AI scene bridges, unrehearsed identity transformations; never canon by default.

**Two mode policy.** For narrative: prioritize legibility of character choices and causality. A transition may disturb space/time only when the scene demonstrates that disturbance. For music video: musical phrase, performance and visual motif may govern the edit, including deliberate discontinuity. Neither mode authorizes generic bright wipes or compulsive presets.

**Baseline restrictions.** Preserve approved character face/body geometry, wardrobe and lens/lighting authorities. No synthetic smoothing, beauty-filter skin, new evidence mechanics, random glitch or unauthorized canon changes. In SECOND HAND specifically, do not revive retired Twelve Photographs, Recorder A/B, predictive audio, evidence puzzles, object displacement or hand-following/tapping as default plot devices. Do not spend paid generation credits without explicit authority.

## Transition selection decision tree
1. Define the semantic delta: same event / changed time / changed place / changed perspective / changed reality / musical re-phrasing.
2. Test a **plain cut** with truthful performance and picture timing. Keep it as the A version.
3. If confusion needs resolution, use continuity/match. If new scene needs anticipation or emotional carry, test J/L sound. If a physical boundary already exists, test an in-camera concealment.
4. Only when the scene *requires* unusual ontology, time experience, or formal musical language consider abstract/analog/3D transitions.
5. If no practical/previs option works and there is a measurable narrative gain, stage a zero-credit neural test and request separate paid authorization.
6. Compare in context, ideally 3–5 seconds on either side, with identical sound and grade before promoting.

## Timing and technique notes
- **No universal duration:** the relevant unit is an edit beat/gesture, not a fixed plugin preset. Where a short blending repair is required, 2–6 frames can be a useful controlled trial for Resolve Smooth Cut, not a mandate for all transitions. [S08]
- **Cinematic frame language:** a 2–6-frame interruption at 24 fps is roughly 83–250 ms; 8–12 frames is roughly 333–500 ms. These are illustrative test ranges only, not automatically tasteful durations.
- **Sound:** listen to transitions without picture; then view the picture without sound. Clean up phase and room tone separately. For music edits, bar/phrase structure usually matters more than a relentless kick-by-kick edit. [S15]
- **Motion:** matching acceleration and blur matters as much as matching direction. Optical-flow interpolation can produce artifacts across dissimilar frames; use original captured movement when possible. [S06,S20]
- **Visual surface:** genuine lens refraction, emulsion artifacts, analog texture and handmade optical processes are useful precisely because they preserve physical irregularity; avoid replacing them with plasticky generic shaders. [S11–S13]

## Full transition registry
### A. Continuity and invisible edits — 14 entries
**TR-A01 — Straight cut** [T0; NLE]. Immediate image replacement without blending. **Best for:** Default for all storytelling; pace and emotional punctuation. **QC trap:** A gratuitous effect would distract.

**TR-A02 — Cut on action** [T0; Capture+NLE]. Join an action across two angles at its most readable motion point. **Best for:** Natural passage between viewpoints. **QC trap:** Direction and limb state must agree.

**TR-A03 — Eyeline match** [T0; Capture+NLE]. Cut from gaze to spatially coherent object or person. **Best for:** Point of view and audience attention. **QC trap:** Axis and geography errors.

**TR-A04 — Shot/reverse-shot** [T0; Capture+NLE]. Alternate viewpoints across established conversation axis. **Best for:** Human performance and relational stakes. **QC trap:** Overcoverage can erase tension.

**TR-A05 — Reaction cut** [T0; Capture+NLE]. Leave action to reveal another person's specific response. **Best for:** Emotional consequence. **QC trap:** Unmotivated reaction misattributes feelings.

**TR-A06 — Cut-in** [T0; Capture+NLE]. Move to a close detail contained in the parent action. **Best for:** Emphasize an object or decision. **QC trap:** False prop continuity.

**TR-A07 — Cutaway** [T0; Capture+NLE]. Temporary detail or parallel view conceals omitted time. **Best for:** Compress dialogue or action. **QC trap:** Cutaway has no story reason.

**TR-A08 — Invisible dark cut** [T0; Capture+NLE]. Cut across full-frame darkness or silhouette. **Best for:** Apparent unbroken movement. **QC trap:** Black levels and grain mismatch.

**TR-A09 — Foreground occlusion cut** [T0; Capture+NLE]. Use a passing body or object to conceal the edit. **Best for:** Unbroken spatial travel. **QC trap:** Occluder speed and shape mismatch.

**TR-A10 — Motion-blur hidden cut** [T0; Capture+NLE]. Place edit during high blur at similar flow direction. **Best for:** Continuous energy across spaces. **QC trap:** Optical-flow artifacts if over-smoothed.

**TR-A11 — Composition hold / delayed cut** [T0; Editorial]. Hold a frame until the dramatic information changes. **Best for:** Tension, observation and unease. **QC trap:** Holding without new information feels slack.

**TR-A12 — Cut after movement** [T0; Editorial]. Wait for action completion rather than cut on impact. **Best for:** Emphasize aftermath and accountability. **QC trap:** Missed emotional reaction.

**TR-A13 — Insert-and-return** [T0; Capture+NLE]. Cut to an insert then return on changed context. **Best for:** Story detail without a full scene change. **QC trap:** Audience loses positional anchor.

**TR-A14 — Reverse angle inversion** [T0; Capture+NLE]. Deliberately swap axis after a clear spatial reset. **Best for:** Alienation or moral reversal. **QC trap:** Accidental 180-degree violation.

### B. Match-cut grammar — 16 entries
**TR-B01 — Graphic match** [T0; Storyboard+NLE]. Align shapes or dominant composition between shots. **Best for:** Elegant association across locations. **QC trap:** Looks ornamental when meanings do not connect.

**TR-B02 — Subject match** [T0; Storyboard+NLE]. Align subject types, poses, profiles or silhouettes. **Best for:** Connect identity, status or roles. **QC trap:** Identity confusion unless intentional.

**TR-B03 — Action match** [T0; Capture+NLE]. Continue a specific movement through the cut. **Best for:** Carry momentum across time and space. **QC trap:** Motion phase mismatch.

**TR-B04 — Direction match** [T0; Storyboard+NLE]. Preserve consistent movement vectors across shots. **Best for:** Uninterrupted pursuit or travel. **QC trap:** Reverse vector reads as bounce.

**TR-B05 — Speed match** [T0; Capture+NLE]. Cut between objects sharing apparent screen velocity. **Best for:** Build kinetic rhyme. **QC trap:** Mismatched shutter/blur.

**TR-B06 — Scale match** [T0; Previs+NLE]. Keep a focal object the same screen size across shots. **Best for:** Conceal change of location or object. **QC trap:** False scale cues break immersion.

**TR-B07 — Color match** [T0; Grade+NLE]. Connect similar chromatic focal regions. **Best for:** Visual rhyme and music sections. **QC trap:** Color as sole reason often feels thin.

**TR-B08 — Luminance match** [T0; Grade+NLE]. Connect highlight/shadow distribution. **Best for:** Smooth tonal movement. **QC trap:** White/black clipping.

**TR-B09 — Texture match** [T0; Capture+NLE]. Align grain, weave, foam, fabric or surface patterns. **Best for:** Tactile associative transition. **QC trap:** VFX smoothness conflicts with physical texture.

**TR-B10 — Eye-position match** [T0; NLE]. Maintain eye coordinates or eye-line direction. **Best for:** Hold attention through character cuts. **QC trap:** Unwanted facial identity blend.

**TR-B11 — Object-function match** [T0; Storyboard+NLE]. Different objects perform the same physical task. **Best for:** Conceptual connection without visual sameness. **QC trap:** Metaphor too obvious.

**TR-B12 — Cause-and-effect match** [T0; Script+NLE]. Cut from action to its distant consequence. **Best for:** Story compression. **QC trap:** Implies causal link not in script.

**TR-B13 — Conceptual / intellectual match** [T0; Editorial]. Juxtapose unlike images that share an idea. **Best for:** Theme or irony. **QC trap:** Vague symbolism.

**TR-B14 — Sound match** [T0; Sound+NLE]. Join matching sound signatures over different sources. **Best for:** Associate locations and behaviors. **QC trap:** Frequency/phase mismatch.

**TR-B15 — Gesture / behavior rhyme** [T0; Performance+NLE]. Match an actor's posture, hesitation or habitual gesture. **Best for:** Character mirroring, imitation or contrast. **QC trap:** May imply supernatural mechanic accidentally.

**TR-B16 — Negative-space match** [T0; Storyboard+NLE]. Connect vacant areas of composition across two shots. **Best for:** Isolation, exclusion, absence. **QC trap:** Subject enters wrong side of frame.

### C. Structural, temporal, and spatial edits — 15 entries

**TR-C07 — Crosscutting** [T0; Editorial]. Alternate actions in separate locations. **Best for:** Parallel stakes or convergence. **QC trap:** False simultaneity.

**TR-C08 — Parallel montage** [T0; Editorial]. Interleave separate events with thematic relation. **Best for:** Contrast people or institutions. **QC trap:** Confuse theme with chronology.

**TR-C09 — Associative montage** [T0; Editorial]. Link images by evolving idea rather than continuity. **Best for:** Visual essay or experimental sequence. **QC trap:** Narrative purpose dissolves.

**TR-C10 — Metric montage** [T0; Editorial]. Organize cut lengths by a measurable progression. **Best for:** Build acceleration or ritual. **QC trap:** Mechanical rhythm feels unmotivated.

**TR-C11 — Rhythmic montage** [T0; Editorial]. Edit to movement patterns inside shots. **Best for:** Dance, violence, group movement. **QC trap:** Only chasing a beat.

**TR-C12 — Tonal montage** [T0; Editorial]. Edit on emotional qualities across images. **Best for:** Change psychological register. **QC trap:** Flat emotional arc.

**TR-C13 — Overtonal montage** [T0; Editorial]. Layer metric, rhythmic and tonal tensions. **Best for:** Intense sequence climax. **QC trap:** Sensory overload.

**TR-C14 — Intellectual montage** [T0; Editorial]. Make a new argument by image collision. **Best for:** Political or philosophical metaphor. **QC trap:** Forced symbolism.

**TR-C15 — Split chronology** [T0; Editorial]. Interlace stages of one event nonlinearly. **Best for:** Subjective recollection. **QC trap:** Time relationships unreadable.

### D. Audio-led transitions — 13 entries
**TR-D01 — J-cut / audio pre-lap** [T0; Sound+NLE]. Bring next shot or scene sound in before the image. **Best for:** Anticipation and narrative bridge. **QC trap:** Reveals location too early.

**TR-D02 — L-cut / audio post-lap** [T0; Sound+NLE]. Let preceding shot's sound outlive its image. **Best for:** Emotional residue and scene linkage. **QC trap:** Tail masks needed new information.

**TR-D03 — Sound bridge** [T0; Sound+NLE]. Carry a common ambience, effect or music bed over the cut. **Best for:** Smooth spatial or emotional shift. **QC trap:** Implausible space if untreated.

**TR-D04 — Audio match cut** [T0; Sound+NLE]. Align related sound events from distinct environments. **Best for:** Connect actions or ideas. **QC trap:** Transient and timbral mismatch.

**TR-D05 — Acoustic morph** [T0; Sound design]. Gradually transform a sound's spectrum or envelope into another. **Best for:** Dream, disorientation, cross-world travel. **QC trap:** Artificial resonances.

**TR-D06 — Reverb-tail bridge** [T0; Sound design]. Carry the decay of a voice or struck object into next image. **Best for:** Psychological aftershock. **QC trap:** Unnatural room acoustics without intent.

**TR-D07 — Hard silence cut** [T0; Sound+NLE]. Remove ambience/music exactly at the image change. **Best for:** Shock, shame, grief or attention. **QC trap:** Codec clicks and dead-space confusion.

**TR-D08 — Ambience swap** [T0; Sound design]. Change environmental bed ahead of or after picture. **Best for:** Signal a new location before reveal. **QC trap:** Brittle ambience jump.

**TR-D09 — Dialogue bridge** [T0; Editorial+Sound]. Complete one conversation's line over a new setting. **Best for:** Reframe meaning. **QC trap:** Implies direct dialog at wrong place.

**TR-D10 — Musical downbeat cut** [T0; Music+NLE]. Cut exactly on a strong musical attack. **Best for:** Energy or section change. **QC trap:** Predictability after repeated use.

**TR-D11 — Anticipation cut** [T0; Music+NLE]. Change picture before a predicted drum or lyric event. **Best for:** Build expectation. **QC trap:** Rhythm feels accidentally early.

**TR-D12 — Afterbeat cut** [T0; Music+NLE]. Delay picture past rhythmic emphasis. **Best for:** Suspense, dislocation, human feeling. **QC trap:** Reads late without creative logic.

**TR-D13 — Sonic perspective shift** [T0; Sound design]. Transition diegetic sound from internal to external viewpoint. **Best for:** Alter relationship to character or space. **QC trap:** Mix perspective not established.

## AI and compositing policy (as of Nov 2026)
- **Kling 4.0:** official guidance supports up to ten image keyframes, 3–30 seconds, and a separate Omni Reference mode. Its dedicated keyframe workflow is not equivalent to an identity-locked shot, and the documentation separates upload/reference abilities by workflow. Good for conceptual multi-stage movement proof, **not automatically final faces**. [S16]
- **Luma Ray3 Modify:** the official material describes start/end steering and character-reference controls for video-to-video, which suits a live-action-guided transition. Treat similarity as a graded QC check rather than a guarantee. Watch cropping and performance drift. [S17]
- **Runway:** prompt guide documents sequential prompting and extending via the last frame of a previous clip; useful for a bridge concept, but seam handling must be done in the edit. [S18]
- **DaVinci / Premiere / After Effects:** do ordinary edits with NLE cuts, sound, mattes and Fusion/AE before risking generative frames. Stock morph/smooth-cut technology is narrowly appropriate for small interview jumps, not identity preservation across different scenes. [S04–S09]
- **Blender:** lock two camera matrices, lens/crop, practical 3D occluder, lights and texture. Capture approved hero frames as the ends; use camera projection only for movement the plates support.

## Safety and QC acceptance rules
- **Editorial intent:** What changes for the viewer? Mark emotion, story question, time, place, point of view, and why a straight cut fails.
- **Source authority:** Record outgoing/incoming exact files, camera fps, framing, approved character and prop references, sound stems, and color space.
- **Geometric lock:** Compare subject location, scale, screen direction, vanishing lines, lens/perspective, focus and camera vector.
- **Performance continuity:** Check readable gesture and eye trace across 6 frames before/after; preserve natural asymmetry; no AI face merge.
- **Optical texture:** Compare halation, grain, highlight rolloff, motion blur/shutter, depth-of-field, negative fill and chromatic aberration.
- **Temporal integrity:** Check dropped/duplicated frames, weird interpolation, legibility of time jump and visual logic for held frames.
- **Audio design:** Review J/L boundaries separately: dialogue intelligibility, ambience perspective, reverb decay, transient clicks, phase and musical structure.
- **Safety/accessibility:** Screen flashes, especially saturated red, rapid strobe and full-frame contrast; provide non-flashing version for risky use.
- **Version comparisons:** Compare A straight cut, B sound-led, C designed transition, all on identical surrounding context and synchronized audio.
- **Human approval:** Director/founder signs narrative candidate before paid generation, final transition QA, canon promotion or broad reuse.

**Flashes:** For online/social distribution, use a conservative no-more-than-three-flashes-in-any-second rule, including saturated red, or verify content is below the applicable thresholds. The W3C notes that qualifying flashes can trigger photosensitive reactions in very short exposure. This does not replace specialist broadcast compliance checks. [S19]

**Never approve** a transition that: changes a canonical face without story permission; invents plot evidence; creates phantom limbs; warps a hand gesture into another gesture without authorization; breaks locked blocking and props; leaks a future story state unintentionally; masks an audio discontinuity with a loud fake riser; requires paid gen without approval; or looks good as a single shot but weakens the scene.

# OVS MULTI-SHOT MATCH SEQUENCING — PRODUCTION ADDENDUM
**OVS-EDIT-TRANSITION-G01A · V001 · November 5, 2026**
**Status:** Studio-method documentation only; project-specific canon, visual authorities, paid generation and production deployment remain separately gated.

## Definition
A multi-shot match sequence (MSMS) has **at least three shots and two consecutive matched cuts**. Every adjacent pair uses a deliberately specified visual, action, camera, behavioral, conceptual or sonic connection. The whole sequence must have a dramatic or musical reason to exist. The match-cut and montage principles are established film grammar; **MSMS and the O-family names are OVS-specific working labels**, not formally recognized universal names.

**Two-layer law:** Every edge must work as a match (A→B, B→C, etc.), AND the whole chain must move through setup → variation → payoff, escalation → release, or a meaningfully closed loop. A six-shot sequence of pretty matching objects that says nothing more than a two-shot edit fails.

## Studio production rules
1. Editorial purpose first: identify precisely what should change in the viewer’s understanding or feeling, or the music’s phrase structure.
2. Plan 3–7 shots as the initial proof target; this is an OVS convention, not an industry standard. Longer chains need editorial justification.
3. Create an explicit shot-to-shot **edge ledger**, with the primary match anchor at every cut, optional secondary anchor, normalized image-space focus point, outgoing/incoming timing, action phase and sonic cue.
4. Choose one macro design: fixed graphic motif, handed-off anchors, alternating dual motifs, intentional broken match or circular return.
5. Plan shot-specific geometry, camera lens, crop, motion vector, subject scale, natural blur, exposure, grain, source framing, identity and wardrobe.
6. Test a 50%-opacity boundary overlay and split-screen or flicker test for each pair; align eye trace and cut on motivated action rather than adding a generic dissolve.
7. Preserve narrative truth and causality: a clever matched gesture cannot create new evidence or a supernatural mechanism unless that is already authorized by the script.
8. For music, use beat and phrase markers from the real audio. Nominal frames per beat = FPS × 60 / BPM; use actual session FPS and audio to avoid fractional-frame drift.
9. Build A = clean-cut edit; B = multi-shot matching edit; C = optional spatial/optical composite. Compare with and without sound. Keep the clean cut if it serves the emotion better.
10. Conventional photography and editorial cuts first; VFX second; generative bridge only T3 / R&D and only under separate explicit approval.

## Six qualification gates
**MSMS-01** Editorial purpose, setup/variation/payoff.
**MSMS-02** Authorized original plates, identity/wardrobe/lens/lighting locks and provenance.
**MSMS-03** Every pairwise edge passes visual focus, movement, audio and match-purpose QC.
**MSMS-04** Whole sequence communicates progression and improves on A / clean-cut comparison.
**MSMS-05** Technical and safety assessment: sync drift, optical flow, temporal flicker, photosensitive flashes, identity drift and unintentional sonic causality.
**MSMS-06** Human approval **for the specific sequence**, recorded with source assets and version. The method’s acceptance does not approve shots.

## New chain architectures (internal OVS taxonomy)
- **TR-O01 Graphic-anchor match chain** [T0; NLE + previsualization]. 3+ consecutive shots preserve one geometry, horizon, or silhouette as the repeated anchor. **Use:** Design a visual motif that moves across places or eras. **Risk:** Local pairwise match succeeds but sequence lacks narrative development.
- **TR-O02 Action / gesture relay** [T0; Performance direction + NLE]. One movement vector or gesture passes from shot to shot through different subjects. **Use:** Compress dispersed actions into a meaningful common behavior. **Risk:** Velocity, screen direction, or intent resets unexpectedly.
- **TR-O03 Behavior / expression relay** [T0; Performance blocking + NLE]. A repeated posture, gaze, hand position, or reaction shifts among performers or moments. **Use:** Link characters through behavior without asserting identical identity. **Risk:** Micro-expression or face identity drift; forced psychology.
- **TR-O04 Camera-vector relay** [T1; Previs + motion control / camera repeat]. Maintain camera trajectory, axis of motion, and focal center across successive shots. **Use:** Suggest a single observation moving through distinct spaces. **Risk:** Parallax, lens perspective and acceleration inconsistent.
- **TR-O05 Scale / perspective relay** [T1; Storyboard + lens tests + NLE]. Match position and size of a subject as actual objects change in scale or focal length. **Use:** Travel from macro to environment while preserving eye trace. **Risk:** Lens distortion or optical size makes match obvious.
- **TR-O06 Practical subject transformation chain** [T1; Props + camera match + optional VFX]. A recognizable object silhouette or function is relayed through multiple practical subjects. **Use:** Evolve a motif without inventing a literal metamorphosis. **Risk:** VFX look, prop continuity, or unrelated substitutions.
- **TR-O07 Era / time-skip graphic chain** [T1; Production design + NLE]. Repeat framing and action across differentiated times, costumes, sets, or locations. **Use:** Compress years or cycles into a legible time progression. **Risk:** Era change indistinguishable; wardrobe and chronology errors.
- **TR-O08 Rhythmic match run** [T0; Music edit + frame timing]. Several matched cuts fall on chosen beats, syncopations, or repeated instrumental attacks. **Use:** Give music a deliberate recurring visual grammar. **Risk:** Mechanical beat spam obscures phrasing and performance.
- **TR-O09 Phrase-length counterpoint relay** [T0; Music edit + performance map]. Match shots at musical phrase boundaries while deliberately varying shot durations within phrases. **Use:** Support long musical structure without every cut landing on a kick. **Risk:** Missed hook/lyric emphasis or rhythm feels arbitrary.
- **TR-O10 Sound-motif multi-shot relay** [T0; Sound editorial + picture editorial]. A related transient, sustained tone, breathing pattern, or timbre bridges successive places. **Use:** Unify a dispersed montage through the sound design. **Risk:** Audio implies an unwanted event or creates spatial confusion.
- **TR-O11 Conceptual escalation chain** [T0; Storyboard + story edit]. Each cut retains a perceivable anchor while the metaphor changes or intensifies. **Use:** Build an argument or emotional progression across shots. **Risk:** A pretty sequence has no escalating information.
- **TR-O12 Deliberate broken-match ending** [T1; Picture edit + sound punctuation]. Establish a predictable matching rule, then violate it once at the intended payoff. **Use:** Represent fracture, refusal, reversal, or interruption. **Risk:** Failure reads accidental or upstages the moment.
- **TR-O13 Circular return / loop closure** [T0; Storyboard + NLE]. Final shot matches opening shape, movement, or action for intentional recurrence. **Use:** Music-video loops, motifs of cycles and repetition. **Risk:** Loop disguises a missing dramatic conclusion.
- **TR-O14 Matched occlusion relay** [T1; In-camera + roto/VFX optional]. Door frames, lens covers, bodies or architectural occluders carry a series between locations. **Use:** Connect separate filmed environments with practical concealment. **Risk:** Unmotivated portals, bad matte edges, lighting mismatch.
- **TR-O15 Parallel-chain interweave** [T1; Editorial + shot ledger]. Two distinct match sequences alternate without losing their own visual rules. **Use:** Compare two journeys, characters, or rhythms while preserving separate identities. **Risk:** Visual anchors cross-contaminate; chronology misread.
- **TR-O16 Neural intermediate chain sandbox** [T3; AI test under human gate]. Use AI only for limited, approved interstitial images between fixed genuine plates. **Use:** R&D where practical match, blocking and compositing cannot deliver. **Risk:** Face, hands, accessories, lens, gravity, provenance or location drift.
